# Outreach kit (draft for owner sign-off)

Goal: the first 3 paying clients (1 at the pilot discount). Promise only what is in `offer.md`.
Track every prospect in a simple sheet: name, company, niche, country, channel, date contacted,
follow-up date, status (new / replied / call / proposal / won / lost), notes.

## Who to talk to first (ideal clients)
| Niche | Typical setup today | Pain we solve | Where to find them |
|---|---|---|---|
| Small marketing/web agencies (2–20 people) | many Zaps/Make scenarios for their own clients, per-task bills | costs grow with every client; nobody watches failures at night | LinkedIn ("agency owner" + "Zapier"/"Make"), agency Slack/Facebook groups, Clutch listings |
| Online stores (Shopify/WooCommerce) | order → sheet/CRM/email flows, review requests, stock alerts | a broken flow = lost orders or angry customers; Zapier tiers jump with order volume | Shopify community, store owner groups, LinkedIn ("founder" + "Shopify") |
| Service businesses (clinics, coaches, real estate) | lead forms → CRM, booking reminders, follow-ups | the "one person who set it up" left; no one knows what runs | local business networks, chambers of commerce, LinkedIn, referrals from accountants/web designers |

Good signals: job posts or LinkedIn posts mentioning Zapier/Make; agencies offering "automation" as a
service; stores with several apps connected; anyone complaining about Zapier price changes.
Platforms: Upwork and Fiverr (worldwide), **Malt** (popular freelance marketplace in France, Germany,
Spain, Netherlands, Belgium) for EU clients.

**First 20 prospects:** 8 agencies, 8 stores, 4 service businesses; at least 10 from your own network
(former colleagues, friends' businesses) — warm contacts reply 5–10× more often than cold ones.

## Rules for contacting people in Europe (not legal advice)
- **Germany, Austria:** marketing emails to businesses generally need prior consent — don't cold-email;
  use LinkedIn messages, referrals, events and your page.
- **Most other EU countries and the UK:** short, relevant B2B emails to a business address are usually
  acceptable if you say who you are, why you write and offer an easy opt-out — check the country's rules.
- Always: one message plus at most two follow-ups, an opt-out line, no bought email lists, delete the data
  of people who say no.

## Discovery call (20 minutes) — questions
1. Which tools do you automate with today? How many Zaps/scenarios are on?
2. What do you pay per month for them (and how has it changed)?
3. Which three automations would hurt most if they stopped for a day?
4. When did one last break? How did you find out? How long until it was fixed?
5. Who builds and fixes them today? Is that person still around?
6. Which apps do they connect (CRM, shop, email, sheets, AI)?
7. Do any handle personal data of customers or patients? Where must data be stored (EU/US)?
8. Do you need several people to log in and edit, or is one admin enough? (CE: one admin login.)
9. How fast do you need a reply when something breaks?
10. Who decides on this, and what would make it a clear yes?
Close: "I'll send a one-page proposal within two days with the flows we'd move, the plan and the price."

## Answers to common objections
- *"Zapier is cheaper for us."* — "For light use, yes. You'd be paying for reliability: a private server,
  tested backups, alerts within minutes and someone who fixes things. If you only need software, Zapier is
  fine."
- *"What if you disappear?"* — "It's open source on your server; we hand over everything (flows, backups,
  settings) and any Activepieces freelancer can take over."
- *"Is it secure?"* — "Your own server, firewall, key-only access, automatic security updates, nightly
  backups in a second location; your app passwords are stored encrypted. EU clients get a data-processing agreement."
- *"We need many users."* — "The free edition has one admin login. If you need team roles, we'll tell you
  honestly that this isn't the right fit yet."
